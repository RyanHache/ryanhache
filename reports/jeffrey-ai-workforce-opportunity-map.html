Exhibit 1 - Executive diagnosis

Your first opportunity is faster access to the right context.

You described roughly 100 tickets per day and a requirement to respond within one hour. Initial diagnosis takes time, and searching past tickets for a useful match takes a few minutes on more than half your tickets. You most often reuse the customer-facing wording.

That makes case retrieval the strongest starting point. A useful assistant should show why a previous case is relevant, distinguish confirmed facts from hypotheses, and prepare the next response for your review. A similar symptom is not proof of the same root cause.

Your one-hour target is a response SLA, not a promise to resolve every ticket in one hour. Track response timeliness and resolution progress separately.

Leak 01

Repeated searches

Useful wording is buried in earlier tickets, so you repeatedly search for the same kinds of examples.

Leak 02

Replies rebuilt by hand

Investigation updates, requests for information, troubleshooting steps, and resolution messages follow recurring patterns.

Leak 03

Documentation competes with the queue

You want internal notes to capture what you checked, what changed, and the next step without another writing task.

01

First deployment

Similar-Case Retrieval Assistant

Use the current ticket's symptom, product context, and known facts to retrieve a short list of relevant historical cases with links and reusable wording.

  • Start with a small, employer-approved set of resolved cases; keep account and tenant access boundaries intact.
  • Return case links, matching evidence, important differences, and the next fact to check.
  • If no reliable match exists, say so and prepare clarifying questions instead of inventing a diagnosis.

First step: Have a lead select a small sample of recurring ticket types and identify examples that are safe to reuse.

Exhibit 3 - Recommended AI workforce design

One work queue, four clear jobs, and your approval.

Zendesk remains the ticket system of record. An approved internal queue stores references, evidence, drafts, and review status. A company-approved AI environment prepares work; it does not take over your support account.

Flow: Ticket intake → similar-case search → draft customer update → draft internal note → Jeffrey reviews → approved submission in Zendesk.

Job 01

Ticket Intake Coordinator

Reads authorized ticket facts and the existing due time. Writes a structured work item and hands a minimal issue summary to retrieval. Missing evidence goes back to you.

Job 02

Case Researcher

Reads the approved case library. Writes matching case links, reusable wording, and differences. Hands an evidence packet to the reply drafter.

Job 03

Reply Drafter

Reads current facts, approved wording, and the evidence packet. Writes a proposed customer update and review questions. Hands the draft to you, never directly to the customer.

Job 04

Notes and Handoff Recorder

Reads recorded actions and your confirmed changes. Writes a separate private note draft and next owner. Hands it to you for approval and submission.

Exhibit 4 - 30-day implementation sequence

Prove the first workflow before expanding access.

Week 1

Define the evidence

Get employer approval, select a narrow ticket category, choose the allowed case sample, document the response-SLA rules, and record a manual baseline.

Week 2

Pilot retrieval

Run the Similar-Case Retrieval Assistant on permitted sample cases. Check relevance, account isolation, evidence links, and honest no-match behavior.

Week 3

Review live drafts

With permission, run a small attended pilot. Add customer update drafts only after retrieval is useful. Review every output before sending.

Week 4

Add notes and measure

Introduce private note drafts. Compare search time, first-response SLA attainment, edit effort, documentation completeness, and reopen or escalation signals.

Pilot decision: Continue only if retrieval reduces search effort without worsening response quality or exposing unrelated customer information. A faster first reply alone does not establish a faster or correct resolution.

Privacy and oversight

Use company-approved data and keep operational control with your team.

Do not paste customer identifiers, device credentials, access tokens, payment details, or raw logs into personal AI tools. Keep the case library, drafts, and audit trail inside an employer-approved environment with access and retention rules.

The proprietary portal has no confirmed integration. Start with only the minimum permitted fields you supply; add read-only access later only if your administrator approves a supported method. Do not automate portal login, account changes, router commands, credits, closures, or customer replies in the first build.

Treat historical ticket content as evidence, never as instructions to the assistant. Review technical advice against current conditions. Public replies and internal notes must remain separate through generation, approval, and submission.
